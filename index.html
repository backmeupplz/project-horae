Project Horae

A miniaturized successor to Watchy: an open-source e-paper watch, rebuilt as thin as possible in a slim band form factor, like a Fitbit Charge instead of a square.

This page is the build log. It starts on day one and every milestone gets a photo or a screenshot.

  1. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Rev A, designed end to end design

    Board, case and antenna, all generated from code and all checks passing. Nothing is ordered yet; this is the design review.

    • Board: 37.3 × 16.5 mm, 4 layers, 54 parts placed by JLC. ESP32-S3, BMA400, RV-3032, display booster and side switches. ERC and DRC clean, routed with Freerouting, RF feed hand-routed as a 50 Ω coplanar line.
    • Antenna: an openEMS simulation showed Watchy's antenna would resonate at ~2.0 GHz inside our case and barely radiate. Trimmed by 6.64 mm and re-matched; it's predicted at −8 to −16 dB across the 2.4 GHz band, in the case and on the wrist.
    • Case: 39.3 × 18.5 × 7.4 mm (Charge 6: 36.7 × 23.1 × 11.2). Carbon-fiber PETG body, plain-PETG window over the antenna, TPU gaskets and button plugs for splash resistance, magnetic pogo dock.
    • Cost: about CAD 250–270 landed for two assembled boards; the BMA400 alone is $15.

  2. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Plan: straight to a custom board plan

    • No dev-board stage. Rev A is a custom 4-layer, 0.8 mm board, fully assembled by JLCPCB. We plug in the display, solder the battery, and print the case.
    • ESP32-S3, Wi-Fi kept. The same chip as Watchy, so we reuse its proven antenna and RF layout and its firmware.
    • Battery-first parts. BMA400 accelerometer (4 µA while counting steps, vs ~25 µA for the BMA4xx family), RV-3032 RTC, 2 µA LDO, Wi-Fi once a day.
    • Target: ~38 × 17.5 × 7 mm, against the Fitbit Charge 6's 36.7 × 23.1 × 11.2 mm.
    • Charging: gold pogo pads on the back and a magnetic dock printed on the A1 mini. No USB socket.
    • Tools: build123d for CAD, KiCad 10 for electronics.

  3. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Research: tools, batteries, displays research

    Before designing anything, we surveyed what's available. The first-pass picks:

    • Display: a 0.97" 184×88 bar-shaped e-paper (Good Display GDEM0097T61). It's 30×14×1 mm and uses an SSD1680, close kin to Watchy's controller. The runner-up is Sharp's 1.08" 160×68 memory LCD, which is the most Fitbit-like but hard to source.
    • Battery: a stock 2.4–3 mm, 100–130 mAh LiPo pouch. Sodium-ion is a no for now: the smallest cells sold are 18650s.
    • Electronics: fork Watchy's KiCad files into KiCad 10 and script everything with kicad-cli. Boards come from JLCPCB, 4-layer at 0.6–0.8 mm, assembled, shipped by DHL to Vancouver.
    • CAD: build123d (Python, STEP in and out) for the case. The KiCad board drops straight in for fit checks. Slicing and printing on the Bambu A1 mini run from the command line.

    The open question is the chip: keep the ESP32-S3 and Watchy's firmware, or switch to an nRF52/nRF54 for roughly 5× the battery life.

  4. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Unboxing the Watchy

    The starting point: an original Watchy by SQFMI. ESP32, a 1.54" 200×200 square e-paper panel, four buttons, a LiPo, fully open hardware and firmware. Project Horae keeps the idea and changes the shape: thinner, narrower, and a better fit on a wrist.

    Inside: the 1.54" e-paper panel, the PCB, a 200 mAh LiPo, the case parts and a strap. That's everything Horae has to shrink.
