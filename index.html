Project Horae

A miniaturized successor to Watchy: an open-source e-paper watch, rebuilt as thin as possible in a slim band form factor, like a Fitbit Charge instead of a square.

This page is the build log. It starts on day one and every milestone gets a photo or a screenshot.

  1. 2026-10-04 · Day 1

    Rev A, slimmer: smaller than a Charge 3 design

    A full review and an "optimize everything" pass, borrowing ideas from Pebble 2, Watchy v3 and Yatchy. The watch is now 35.6 × 18.0 × 7.05 mm: smaller than a Fitbit Charge 3 (38 × 18.3 × 11.8) in every direction, and 40% thinner. Day 0 was 40.5 × 18.5 × 7.15; an interim pass capped at the Charge 6 length was 36.7 × 20.0 × 7.45.

    • Board: 33.8 × 16.2 × 0.6 mm, 4 layers, routed and DRC-clean with 0 warnings. The tallest part is now 1.0 mm, down from 1.2.
      • The RTC chip is gone: a 32 kHz crystal runs the ESP32's own clock, as on Watchy v3 and Yatchy, saving $3.44.
      • The charger is now a TI BQ25101 (75 nA drain, 28 V-tolerant input) and the regulator a TI TPS62840 buck (60 nA quiescent), replacing a 2 µA LDO.
      • The e-paper booster now uses Solomon's reference values in 1.0 × 0.6 mm packages.
      • The display and I²C lines are on low-power GPIOs, so the ULP core can redraw the minutes without waking the main CPU (Yatchy's trick).
    • Case: the strap lugs were rendering detached from the body; they are now one piece with the side walls. Standard 16 mm spring-bar straps, 43.1 mm lug to lug. Still single-filament PETG with TPU gaskets, plus an optional 6.55 mm version for a 2.5 mm cell.
    • Corrections from the review:
      • JLC's cheap "Economic" assembly can't place 0201 parts, so this board needs Standard assembly. Two assembled boards come to about USD 165–180 before shipping and tax, not 120–130.
      • Real batteries for this pocket hold 40–55 mAh, not ~100. That's still about 5–7 weeks with good firmware.
      • No 6 mm vibration motor with spring contacts exists, so the battery and motor take 4 solder joints.
    • Antenna: re-simulated in openEMS with the new 0.6 mm board, the slim case, both steel spring bars and the strap. Watchy's trimmed antenna stays as is; a new match (0.3 pF / 1.2 nH) gives −9 to −15 dB across the 2.4 GHz band in the case and −9 to −14 dB on the wrist. The simulation also caught a script bug: the ground pours on three layers had stopped 7.5 mm short of the antenna. Fixed.

  2. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Rev A, designed end to end design

    Board, case and antenna, all generated from code, with every check passing. Nothing is ordered yet; this is the design review, and it has already been through several rounds.

    • Board: 38.5 × 16.5 mm, 4 layers, 54 parts placed by JLC.
      • Chips: ESP32-S3, an ST LIS2DUX12 accelerometer (step counting at 2.7 µA), an RV-3032 RTC and the e-paper booster.
      • Four capacitive touch pads instead of switches, a 6 mm coin motor on spring contacts, and a top-port PDM mic that draws 0 µA when off.
      • ERC and DRC clean. The RF feed is hand-routed as a 50 Ω coplanar line.
    • Antenna: an openEMS simulation showed Watchy's antenna would resonate at ~2.0 GHz inside our case and barely radiate. Trimmed by 6.64 mm and re-matched, it's predicted at −8 to −16 dB across the 2.4 GHz band. A re-run with the final case shape and the steel spring bar is still to do.
    • Case: 40.5 × 18.5 × 7.15 mm (Charge 6: 36.7 × 23.1 × 11.2).
      • "Pebble" shape: carbon-fiber PETG body, with a plain-PETG window over the antenna and solid windows over the touch pads.
      • Splash resistance from TPU gaskets, plus a vented mic duct.
      • Standard 16 mm spring-bar straps and a magnetic pogo dock. The battery is the full 25 × 16 × 2.5 mm.
    • Cost: about USD 120–130 for two assembled boards before shipping and tax. Parts are $14.61 per board after swapping the $15 BMA400 for the $4 LIS2DUX12.

  3. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Plan: straight to a custom board plan

    • No dev-board stage. Rev A is a custom 4-layer, 0.8 mm board, fully assembled by JLCPCB. We plug in the display, solder the battery, and print the case.
    • ESP32-S3, Wi-Fi kept. The same chip as Watchy, so we reuse its proven antenna and RF layout and its firmware.
    • Battery-first parts. BMA400 accelerometer (4 µA while counting steps, vs ~25 µA for the BMA4xx family), RV-3032 RTC, 2 µA LDO, Wi-Fi once a day.
    • Target: ~38 × 17.5 × 7 mm, against the Fitbit Charge 6's 36.7 × 23.1 × 11.2 mm.
    • Charging: gold pogo pads on the back and a magnetic dock printed on the A1 mini. No USB socket.
    • Tools: build123d for CAD, KiCad 10 for electronics.

  4. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Research: tools, batteries, displays research

    Before designing anything, we surveyed what's available. The first-pass picks:

    • Display: a 0.97" 184×88 bar-shaped e-paper (Good Display GDEM0097T61). It's 30×14×1 mm and uses an SSD1680, close kin to Watchy's controller. The runner-up is Sharp's 1.08" 160×68 memory LCD, which is the most Fitbit-like but hard to source.
    • Battery: a stock 2.4–3 mm, 100–130 mAh LiPo pouch. Sodium-ion is a no for now: the smallest cells sold are 18650s.
    • Electronics: fork Watchy's KiCad files into KiCad 10 and script everything with kicad-cli. Boards come from JLCPCB, 4-layer at 0.6–0.8 mm, assembled, shipped by DHL to Vancouver.
    • CAD: build123d (Python, STEP in and out) for the case. The KiCad board drops straight in for fit checks. Slicing and printing on the Bambu A1 mini run from the command line.

    The open question is the chip: keep the ESP32-S3 and Watchy's firmware, or switch to an nRF52/nRF54 for roughly 5× the battery life.

  5. 2026-10-03 · Day 0

    Unboxing the Watchy

    The starting point: an original Watchy by SQFMI. ESP32, a 1.54" 200×200 square e-paper panel, four buttons, a LiPo, fully open hardware and firmware. Project Horae keeps the idea and changes the shape: thinner, narrower, and a better fit on a wrist.

    Inside: the 1.54" e-paper panel, the PCB, a 200 mAh LiPo, the case parts and a strap. That's everything Horae has to shrink.
